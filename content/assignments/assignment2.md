Conduct your own sound survey. You will listen to the same scene 3 times (twice
in person and once to a recording that you made). Each time you listen, you
will make a written reflection. Finally, you’ll write an analysis on your
reflections.

## Instructions

Listen to the same scene on two different occasions. On one of the two
occasions, record the sound, then listen to the recording later. Each time you
listen, write a 200-400 word reflection. After you’ve finished all of your
listening and reflections, read your reflections and write a final 200-400
analysis. The completed assignment is four short pieces of writing.

## Selecting a scene

Choose a place that you find sonically interesting.

What’s “sonically interesting”? You have the power to decide that, but here are
some ideas:

- An acoustically unusual or novel environment (e.g. very echo-y or
  reverberant, very quiet, confusing or disorienting sonically)
- A space where the sound has a strong emotional affect or resonance
- A space in which there is a mismatch between the sounds and their sources
  (e.g. without the visual, it is difficult to figure out what the sound even is)
- A space whose sounds evoke a memory, visual metaphor, concept, or myth

## Listening Technique

Try to shut out other stimuli and just listen. Close your eyes if you are able.
Keep a paper and pencil nearby to jot notes.

Space out the visits/listening sessions by at least 6 hours. Try to visit on
different days or different times of day.

On one one of the two visits, make a recording of the scene. You can use your
phone as your recorder. Then, listen to it later on headphones.

As you listen, imagine the sound in three modalities:

- As a space. The sound you are hearing is the sound of a particular
  environment. What does the sound by itself tell you about the space you are
  inhabiting?
- As a material. What is the literal substance of this sound? In contrast the
  first modality (sound as space), we’re thinking here about the sound in the
  abstract, divorced from its source or environment.
- As a representation. What might this sound represent? These might be
  emotions, themes, concepts, other spaces, objects, people, animals.

## Reflections

Immediately after each listening session, make work in reflection.

You may write 200-400 words. Refer to Assignment 1’s instructions for
inspiration. For listening sessions 2 and 3, focus in particular on comparison:

What sensations did you discover while listening according to the three
modalities noted above?

- How did your experience change from session to session?
- To what extent can you attribute the varied experiences to changes in the
- sound versus changes in your listening?
- Analysis
- After you’ve listened and reflected, take another day or so, then read your
  reflections and analyze them. The goal here is to “zoom out” and interpret your
  own sensations. In what ways were each of the listening experiences similar? In
  what ways were they different? What did the listening exercise teach you about
  sound and about yourself as a listener/perceiver?
