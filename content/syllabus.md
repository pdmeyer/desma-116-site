## Course Description

Sonic Media is an introduction to sound as an artistic medium. Students will
learn to listen to sounds, think about sounds, describe and discuss sounds,
make sounds, and use sounds in multimedia artworks.

This course is rooted in a practice of critical listening. We will begin by
tuning our brains and bodies to listen perceptively and critically. We will
explore some theoretical frameworks for listening and learn the skill of
focused listening. As we explore the theory of practice of using sound within
artworks, we will rely upon our listening skills.

As we listen, we will ask questions. What information does sound give us about
time and place? How does it effect the emotional tone of a scene or space? What
techniques do media artists and filmmakers, and musicians use to communicate
through sound?

Finally, we will develop practical skills that allow us to make work with
sound. Students will develop an understanding of the basic physics of sound,
touching upon acoustics, psychoacoustics, analog and digital audio signal
processing. They will acquire fundamental techniques for capturing sounds in
the real world and synthesize sounds with hardware and software.

Students will complete Sonic Media with the skills and experience to listen
perceptively and use sound expressively. They will finish the quarter with a
working knowledge of techniques in field recording, physical sound design,
sound editing, synthesis, and sound design in software. Over the course of the
quarter, students will create a library of sounds ready for use in future
projects.

This is not a class on music-making. We acknowledge that any assemblage of
sounds can be considered music, and that skills in critical listening and sound
design are instrumental to contemporary music-making. But the music that most
of us listen to most of the time relies on concepts, techniques, and traditions
that we will not explore in this course. While students are not forbidden from
making rhythmic or melodic music in this course, our focus is both more narrow
and more broad. By focusing narrowly on sound as a material, we equip ourselves
to use sound in a wide range of artistic contexts.

## Expectations

Enthusiasm and a willingness to explore sounds is the most important
requirement. Attendance and participation are also critical. Students are
expected to engage with the course leaders and with one another, to express and
critique ideas, to support and challenge themselves and one another. This class
is a safe space to take creative risks and be weird, which means that each of
us must be aware and respectful of the range of backgrounds, identities, and
points of view that will reveal themselves.

### Teaching Assistant

The teaching assistant for this course is Roxanne Harris aka alsoknownasrox.

Roxanne Harris “alsoknownasrox” is an artist-researcher and musician-programmer
born and raised in New York City. Approaching computation as a site of
collective reckoning, she uses code to parameterize and transfigure sound in
unreal time, extending improvisational dexterity across mutable architecture.
Through procedural impromptu, her work traces the interval between imagined
sound and realized consequence, foregrounding gestural memory through runtime
thought.

### Assignments

This course includes reading/listening/watching to prepare for each class, plus
assignments to explore listening and sound work.

Students are expected to complete the reading/listening/watching before class,
and to be prepared with impressions, questions, or conclusions for discussion
in class.

Assignments should be uploaded to the course file system _before class_ on the
day that the assignment is due. As with the readings, please be prepared to
share a bit about your work and discuss it in class. If there is a reason you
won’t be able to submit an assignment on time, let me know by email as soon as
possible. Repeated failure to turn in assignments on time will impact your
grade in this course!

You can think of the assignments in this course as one big project, broken up
into sections. The first 3 assignments are about training our listening skills
and our ability to think critically about sound. The remaining assignments will
ask you to create sounds of your own.

Please submit assignments using the Google Drive link:
[https://forms.gle/v2XLw1hevoNFsceU7](https://forms.gle/v2XLw1hevoNFsceU7)

Assignments over the course of the quarter will focus on the topics of:

- Developing and exercising critical listening skills
- Relating sound to other media (image, video, text)
- Recording and editing sounds in the real world
- Synthesizing and transforming sounds in the completely
- Composing and arranging sounds temporally

### Late Work

You are expected to turn in work on time. I would prefer that you turn in late
work on time than turn in work late. Believe it or not, deadlines are one of
the great gifts a program like this offers you.

If at the time that work is assigned you anticipate that you will need more
time, let me know. We are able to rearrange due dates in order to work with
your schedule, but this _must_ happen at the time at which the work is
assigned.

Assignments in this class are graded on a scale from 1 - 10. Each day that the
work is late results in a 1-point reduction in your grade.

### Grading

Grading breaks down into two categories:

- Participation and Attendance
- Assignments

#### Participation and Attendance (20%)

This is all about in-class engagement. Being an active participant during class
sessions helps you and others get the most of out of the course. Participation
does not look the same for everybody. For some, active participant may mean
playing a constructive and active role in critiques, but participation can also
be more subtle. For example, a student who is less outspoken but who supports
and inspires their peers in more group projects would also receive full marks
for class participation. If you would like to talk about what participation
means to you, please come see me.

#### Assignments (80% total)

- Assignments 1 - 3: 5% each (15% total)
- Assignments 4 - 6: 10% each (30% total)
- Final Project: 35%

#### Assignment Grading Criteria

Everybody is able to get a high grade in this class by demonstrating a genuine
commitment to learning and growing. We are all able to recognize work that has
been made with care, humility, and a respect for the viewer/listener.
Risk-taking is encouraged in this course and “failure” is completely compatible
with high marks. What is _incompatible_ are apathy and cynicism.

If you have any questions about the approach to grading or want to discuss your
grade on any particular assignment, please email me or visit office hours.

### Attendance Policy

Attendance is critical in this course. If you believe that you will need to be
absent from class, it is your responsibility to let us know. If you think you
may not need to make it, send an email to me and our TA letting us know which
day(s) you think you will be absent and the reason. I will email you back to
confirm whether the absence would be considered excused or unexcused.

If you are unwell, please use the Ashe Center's online system for generating a
medical note. Self-generated notes will generally be accepted, within reason.
Please use medical notices responsibly. Being ill and not able to attend class
does not excuse you from any course responsibilities, including assignments or
class participation.

#### Excused Absences

Valid conditions for excusal are time-sensitive medical or mental health needs
(must include a note from the doctor or mental health professional), family
emergencies, and acts of god. Deadlines and workload for this and other classes
is not a valid condition for excusal.

**Unexcused Absences** Unexcused absences will lower your grade.

- 2 unexcused absence will lower the final grade by 1/3 of a letter (a B+
  becomes a B)
- 3 unexcused absences lower the final grade by a 2/3 letter (a B+ becomes a
  B-)
- 4 unexcused absences lower the final grade by a full letter (a B+ becomes a
  C)
- 5+ unexcused absences lower the final grade to an F.

**Total Absences** A total of 6 or more absences of any kind will result in an
Failing grade for the class. Certain extenuating circumstances (such as a
prolonged illness) that prevent completion of a final project may result in an
Incomplete grade.

**Tardiness** There will be a 5 minute grace period after the starting time of
the class. Arriving within this grace period is considered on time. Arriving
between 5 and 20 minutes late is considered "late" for grading purposes.
Arriving more than 20 minutes late is considered an absence. Three late marks
equate to one absence. Exceptions are granted only if you contact me. If you
think you might be late for class, send an email!

### Technology Policy

Use of phones and laptops in class follows the general guidelines around class
engagement and participation. Students who are engaged in class discussions
will receive high marks for the participation component of their grade. Please
make sure phones and laptops are not a distraction during our class and studio
time.

#### AI Policy

There is little value to be gleaned from the use of AI tools in this course.
Writing assignments, all of which center your human response to artworks, are
expected to be completed entirely without the assistance of AI tools. Use of AI
tools such as chatbots to assist you in the process of learning technical
concepts like the physics of sound, theory of digital audio, and hardware and
software tool is allowed. Under no circumstances should AI tools be used to
generate text, audio, or visual materials for class assignments. Doing so will
be considered academic misconduct. If you have any questions about what
constitutes acceptable use of AI tools, please consult with the instructor
before submitting your work.

## Context

### Commitment to Diversity and Safer Spaces

We understand the classroom as a space for practicing freedom; where one may
challenge psychic, social, and cultural borders and create meaningful artistic
expressions. To do so we must acknowledge and embrace the different identities
and backgrounds we inhabit. This means that we will use preferred pronouns,
respect self-identifications, and be mindful of special needs. Disagreement is
encouraged and supported, however our differences affect our conceptualization
and experience of reality, and it is extremely important to remember that
certain gender, race, sex, and class identities are more privileged while
others are undermined and marginalized. Consequently, this makes some people
feel more protected or vulnerable during debates and discussions. A
collaborative effort between the students, TA, and instructor is needed to
create a supportive learning environment. While everyone should feel free to
experiment creatively and conceptually, if a class member points out that
something you have said or shared with the group is offensive, avoid being
defensive; instead approach the discussion as a valuable opportunity for us to
grow and learn from one another. Alternatively if you feel that something said
in discussion or included in a piece of work is harmful, you are encouraged to
speak with the instructor or TA. (from
[voidLab](https://github.com/voidlab/diversity-statement))
